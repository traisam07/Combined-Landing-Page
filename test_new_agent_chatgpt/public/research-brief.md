# PulseMind — Exploratory research brief

PulseMind is a proof-of-concept clinical decision-support dashboard for adult mechanically ventilated ICU patients. It explores how respiratory information, trends, contributing variables, and data limitations can be organized for clinician review.

## Updated survey findings

The supplied updated Survey Result.md supersedes the survey figures in the earlier project report.

- **36 eligible respondents:** 22 ICU nurses and 14 ICU physicians. All reported direct experience caring for, assessing, or treating mechanically ventilated adults within the previous 24 months.
- **4.25 of 6 source groups:** the average number of comparable information-source groups selected per respondent. This measures the breadth of sources selected, not time spent, review difficulty, or technical fragmentation.
- **28 of 36 (77.8%):** rated the concept useful or very useful if validated for safety and appropriate for clinical workflows. This is conditional concept acceptance, not satisfaction after using the product or evidence of effectiveness.

## Method and interpretation

The analysis combines two survey datasets (N=22 and N=14) only where their variables are comparable. Different barrier scales and open-ended versus prompted responses are not pooled. Prototype feedback comes only from the N=14 subgroup.

The sample is small and exploratory. Sampling frames, invitation counts, and response rates are unavailable, so representativeness cannot be assessed. There are no patient outcome data, task-completion measures, or prototype usage logs in the survey analysis.

The results support further investigation of multi-source information organization, visible timelines and data freshness, understandable explanations, and clinician control. They do not establish clinical performance, validated usability, clinical effectiveness, adoption, or willingness to pay.

## Current development stage

The prototype uses simulated or file-based information and is not connected to live hospital systems. Its scores and assessments are illustrative, not clinically validated. The landing-page interaction is an independent demonstration using synthetic examples; it is not the original clinical dashboard.

PulseMind does not diagnose, recommend treatment, control ventilators, or replace existing alarms or clinical judgment. The next milestones are a clinician-approved prediction target and horizon, a suitable retrospective dataset, and a locked validation protocol. Hospital-authorized shadow-mode evaluation would require separate clinical, technical, security, workflow, and governance approval.
