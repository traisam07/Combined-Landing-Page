§01 — The problem

The ICU isn’t short of data.

It’s spread across six places, sampled at different rates, and interrupted by alarms. In our survey, a single respiratory review drew on 4.25 of 6 source groups on average.

It’s short of a clear view.

PulseMind lines every source up on one patient timeline, then reads the trajectory instead of the snapshot. The device alarms stay exactly where they are.

  1. 01 / 07

    36 ICU professionals.

    22 nurses and 14 physicians at two hospitals in Ho Chi Minh City. Every one of them had directly cared for a mechanically ventilated adult in the past 24 months.

  2. 02 / 07

    Everyone starts at the ventilator. Then the hunt begins.

    All 36 use the ventilator screen and its alarms to assess respiratory change. Most then piece together everything else:

    • Ventilator screen & alarms36
    • Direct examination35
    • Bedside monitor33
    • Blood gas & labs21
    • Charts & flowsheets17
    • Handover & colleagues11

    Average: 4.25 of 6 source groups per respondent.

  3. 03 / 07 · Hospital A

    Alarms dominate the work.

    Asked what affects their monitoring most, 20 of 22 at Hospital A described ventilator alarms or abnormal ventilator parameters. The other two named staffing.

  4. 04 / 07 · Hospital B

    Too many places to look.

    8 of 14 rated checking many devices and records as a major barrier (4–5 on a 5-point scale). As many said the same of balancing documentation with monitoring, and 7 of 14 of tracking down older readings.

  5. 05 / 07 · Hospital A

    Their biggest fear? More prompts.

    Asked what would worry them about a tool like PulseMind, every valid answer (21 of 21) flagged too many prompts. So PulseMind only prompts when defined criteria are met.

  6. 06 / 07 · Hospital B

    Keep what already works.

    All 14 want the existing ventilator and monitor alarms kept, along with routine bedside nursing observation. PulseMind adds a view. It removes nothing.

  7. 07 / 07 · Both hospitals

    A conditional yes.

    28 of 36 rated the concept useful or very useful, provided it’s shown to be safe and fits their workflow. The other 8 rated it moderately useful.

Exploratory, descriptive survey (N=36). Hospitals A (n=22) and B (n=14) used different questionnaires, so results are pooled only where the questions match. Each dot is one respondent; dot positions are illustrative. These findings guide design. They are not evidence of clinical effectiveness.

§04 — How it works

From fragments toone focused prompt.

  1. 01 — Align

    Organise data over time

    Checks there’s enough data to assess, flags what’s missing, and lines every source up on one patient chronology, whatever its sampling rate.

  2. 02 — Analyse

    Read the trajectory

    Instead of isolated readings, it derives features across time windows, such as recent values, rates of change, variability and relationships between variables, alongside patient context.

  3. 03 — Estimate

    A specific, explainable risk

    A target-specific model estimates the likelihood of one defined respiratory event within a stated horizon, and ranks the variables that most influenced it. These influences are not presented as causes.

  4. 04 — Prompt

    Only when it matters

    Only High or Critical assessments generate a focused prompt with the target, horizon, trend, contributors, data limits and a plain-language explanation. Everything else stays quietly on the board.

§06 — Boundaries

The fine print,in large print.

Struck from the spec,
permanently

  • Diagnosis.
  • Treatment recommendations.
  • Ventilator control.
  • Silencing or replacing alarms.
  • Replacing clinical judgement.

Read-only, by design

No path back to the bedside.

There is no pathway for sending commands, ventilator settings or treatment instructions to any device or source system. This holds for the prototype and for any future integrated version. Every clinical action happens outside PulseMind, through existing hospital workflows.

Honest about gaps

It tells you what it doesn’t know.

  • Risk level unavailable

    No score is guessed when required inputs are missing.

  • Data sufficiency unknown

    Uncertain inputs are flagged, never left blank.

  • Carried forward · 18 min

    Repeated values say when they were last actually measured.

  • Population reference

    Filled-in values are labelled as filled in.

Specific, never vague

Not “this patient is deteriorating.”

Every assessment names the event it estimates, the prediction horizon, the assessment time and the time of the latest usable data. Its risk level is about that target only, not the patient’s overall severity.

Explanation layer

An LLM explanation layer turns structured outputs into plain clinician-facing language after prediction. It never sets or changes the score. Citations come only from approved, version-controlled clinical documents.

What isn’t locked yet

The clinical target, horizon, cohort, outcome label, features and thresholds will be fixed with clinical partners first. Until then, prototype scores are illustrative, not calibrated probabilities.

Intended integration · one verified pathway per hospital, not a cable to every bed

§08 — Roadmap

Six phases.Every gate can say stop.

Each phase answers one uncertainty, then decides whether to proceed, repeat, change direction or stop.

  1. Phase 1 We are here

    Public-data proof of concept

    Define the first target and horizon, build an initial model on MIMIC-IV, and connect it to an early dashboard.

  2. Phase 2

    Local evaluation

    Test the model unchanged on approved, de-identified historical data from a Vietnamese hospital. Refine it only if needed.

  3. Phase 3

    Live integration MVP

    Prove reliable data from live ICU equipment, such as a HAMILTON-C6 or S1, through an approved pathway.

  4. Phase 4

    Primary dataset

    Link new patients’ data to confirmed respiratory events. Train, test on separate records, then lock the model.

  5. Phase 5

    Shadow test & pilot

    Run hidden predictions on live data first. If the results hold, run a controlled clinician pilot. PulseMind stays read-only throughout.

  6. Phase 6

    Deployment readiness

    Put security, monitoring, support and update processes in place for a staged rollout across hospitals.

Next milestone

Not a deployment. A clinician-approved prediction target, a usable retrospective dataset and a locked validation protocol.
